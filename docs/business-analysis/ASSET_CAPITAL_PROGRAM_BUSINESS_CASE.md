# Asset Capital Programme Business Case

## Purpose and evidence boundary

This is an investment-committee-style business case for the repository's
synthetic cross-service asset portfolio. It shows how a business analyst can
turn asset, lifecycle, cost, risk, GIS, funding, delivery, and governance
evidence into a controlled decision.

The decision is simulated. All asset records, costs, locations, assumptions,
roles, dates, risks, and targets are synthetic. Nothing in this document is an
engineering recommendation, an approved municipal capital plan, authority to
spend, or a claim of municipal project experience.

## Decision requested

Authorize **Option 2 — Risk-based renewal** to proceed through structured
condition, scope, service-impact, whole-life option, affordability, funding,
delivery, climate, accessibility, equity, safety, procurement, and stakeholder
validation.

Do not authorize procurement or construction until the evidence gates in this
case are complete and the accountable approval body accepts the preferred
option and residual risks.

## Case for change

The default evidence base contains 96 asset records across Water, Stormwater,
Roads, Facilities, Fleet, and Parks. It deliberately routes nine materially
defective records to data remediation. Of the remaining records, 78 are both
planning-ready and in service. They represent:

- $110.586M of synthetic replacement value;
- $2.547M of annualized renewal need;
- 44 high or very-high condition × criticality risks; and
- 55 non-monitor capital candidates with $37.950M of source treatment screens.

A priority list alone is not a business case. Decision-makers also need to know
which alternatives were considered, what annual cash and delivery constraints
apply, how volatile assumptions change the result, how funding could be
assembled, who owns each risk and benefit, and what evidence is required at
each approval gate. The live app adds that decision layer without presenting
uncertain assumptions as facts.

## Objectives and success tests

| Objective | Success test | Accountable role |
|---|---|---|
| Make the capital choice explainable | Every candidate has a traceable owner, source evidence, risk, cost screen, priority, disposition, and decision record | Asset information owner |
| Protect affordability | No approved annual programme exceeds its funding ceiling without authorized change control | Finance lead |
| Focus scarce validation capacity | Every scheduled high or very-high asset is validated or formally deferred with rationale before commitment | Engineering lead |
| Make delivery credible | Every released project passes scope, funding, procurement, capacity, dependency, and continuity gates | Programme sponsor |
| Protect public value | Safety, service, accessibility, climate, environmental, equity, and community impacts are evidenced before option approval | Service owner |
| Sustain the outcome | Every completed project has commissioning, asset/GIS update, operational handover, and benefits evidence | Service owner |

Targets are governance commitments for the simulated case. They are not claims
of achieved benefits.

## Options and default planning screen

The live board recalculates these results when the service scope, risk focus,
annual envelope, horizon, escalation, contingency, discount rate, delivery
capacity, or funding mix changes.

Default basis: ten years from 2027; $8.0M annual envelope; 3.5% annual cost
escalation; 15% planning contingency; 4% capital discount rate; and capacity
for 14 projects per year.

| Option | Decision posture | Annual envelope | Capacity / year | Assets scheduled | High / very-high scheduled | Nominal capital | Capital PV | Candidates beyond horizon | Deferred annualized risk exposure |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 — Minimum response | Constrain new commitments and validate only the highest priorities | $5.2M | 8 | 54 | 38 | $44.604M | $36.833M | 1 | $3.009M |
| 2 — Risk-based renewal | Use the selected envelope and a governed, risk-ordered delivery plan | $8.0M | 14 | 55 | 39 | $47.458M | $41.485M | 0 | $0 |
| 3 — Accelerated resilience | Increase funding and capacity to advance more work | $10.0M | 18 | 55 | 39 | $46.460M | $41.605M | 0 | $0 |

**Screening recommendation:** take Option 2 to validation. It materially reduces
the deferred portfolio compared with Option 1 without assuming the larger
funding and delivery expansion required by Option 3.

## Service outcomes and lifecycle choices

The app now carries one synthetic technical and one synthetic customer service
measure for Water, Stormwater, Roads, Facilities, Fleet, and Parks. Each record
states the outcome, target direction, current planning result, target,
consequence of shortfall, and accountable role. The view joins those service
gaps to the selected assets, high/very-high risk count, replacement value, and
annual renewal pressure. These values demonstrate the governance structure;
they are not adopted municipal standards or City of Fernie results.

Every service must compare four strategies before Gate 2 selects a preferred
asset solution:

1. operate and maintain;
2. rehabilitate;
3. renew or replace; and
4. a service-specific non-infrastructure solution.

The catalogue states the service response, cost/risk question, and evidence
required for each strategy. It intentionally does not manufacture a preferred
option or whole-life cost before engineering and service evidence exists.

This is not yet a whole-life economic appraisal. The capital PV discounts
screened project costs only. Operating costs, residual values, service
benefits, carbon, monetized risk reduction, and distributional impacts must be
validated before a benefit-cost or net-present-value claim is made.

## Sensitivity and decision triggers

The app tests five reproducible cases: the base screen, construction costs 15%
above the planning basis, a 10% annual-envelope reduction, no bridge financing
for the indicative grant share, and a combined cost/funding stress.

Reopen the preferred option when any of the following occurs:

- the cost estimate or scope changes beyond the approved tolerance;
- grant eligibility, timing, or matching requirements become uncertain;
- the annual resource plan cannot support the screened project volume;
- a condition assessment changes risk, intervention, or urgency;
- a safety, service, accessibility, climate, environmental, equity, statutory,
  or community impact changes the option comparison; or
- procurement, permits, dependencies, or construction windows move a project
  outside the screened year.

## Financial and funding case

The default app assumption allocates screened capital indicatively across 50%
reserves/current revenue, 20% grants/contributions, and 30% debt/other
financing. The mix always reconciles to the plan but is not presented as
eligible or authorized.

Finance validation must confirm:

- annual cash flow and competing commitments;
- reserve policy and minimum balances;
- grant programme fit, eligible cost, deadlines, matching share, and fallback;
- borrowing authority, debt servicing, and any public approval requirements;
- operating-budget consequences and rate/tax impacts;
- cost-estimate class, escalation, contingency, and change thresholds; and
- how project bundling or deferral changes cost, risk, and service outcomes.

## Commercial and procurement case

Before authorizing a procurement, the team must document market capacity,
packaging strategy, delivery model, evaluation criteria, conflicts and fairness
controls, insurance and bonding, performance security, accessibility and
environmental requirements, data and GIS handover, warranty, commissioning,
and vendor-performance measures.

The related
[public-sector decision and procurement brief](PUBLIC_SECTOR_DECISION_AND_PROCUREMENT_BRIEF.md)
provides the controlled demonstration, weighted evaluation, policy-impact,
and stage-gate pattern. Local legislation, bylaws, delegation limits,
procurement policy, and records requirements must replace the neutral portfolio
assumptions before use.

## Programme risk register

The downloadable case carries nine named risks. Each has a description,
likelihood, impact, level, mitigation, accountable role, and status:

1. condition or treatment-scope uncertainty;
2. construction cost escalation;
3. grant eligibility or timing;
4. internal and market delivery capacity;
5. procurement and long-lead supply;
6. service continuity and safety;
7. climate and environmental under-design;
8. asset, inspection, cost, and GIS information ownership; and
9. accessibility, equity, and community impact.

These risks remain open or in treatment. The model does not convert a proposed
mitigation into an achieved control.

## Stakeholders and decision rights

| Role | Accountable contribution |
|---|---|
| Approval body / Council-equivalent | Strategic direction, funding authority, accepted residual risk |
| Executive sponsor | Case ownership, scope, criteria, escalation, stage-gate recommendation |
| Finance lead | Affordability, funding, cash flow, reserves/debt, sensitivity, financial controls |
| Engineering lead | Condition, alternatives, design basis, cost/schedule confidence, technical acceptance |
| Service owners | Criticality, service levels, continuity, operating impact, benefits, handover |
| Asset information / GIS owners | Identity, lineage, geometry, data quality, system-of-record updates |
| Procurement lead | Market approach, evaluation, contract, fairness, vendor performance |
| Safety, accessibility, climate, environment, and equity advisors | Mandatory impact screens and acceptance evidence |
| Community and affected users | Needs, impacts, trade-offs, access, disruption, and response evidence |
| Programme manager / business analyst | Requirements, options, dependencies, RAID, decisions, reporting, change, traceability |

Named individuals, local delegations, and meeting cadence must be confirmed in
the project charter and RACI before Gate 1 closes.

## Delivery and change case

| Gate | Evidence required | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| 0 — Mandate and governance | Sponsor, decision owner, scope, criteria, RACI, records location | Approve discovery mandate |
| 1 — Evidence validation | Condition, service consequence, ownership, GIS, data exceptions | Accept evidence baseline |
| 2 — Options and business case | Whole-life options, funding, affordability, impact screens, engagement plan | Select preferred option |
| 3 — Design and procurement readiness | Design, packaging, market route, permits, continuity, safety, capacity | Authorize procurement |
| 4 — Tranche delivery | Approved scope/cost/schedule, RAID, change control, communications | Release each tranche |
| 5 — Commission and realize benefits | Acceptance, asset/GIS update, training, handover, benefits, lessons | Close and sustain |

Change management is part of delivery rather than a final communication step.
Each tranche needs impacted-role analysis, communication, training, procedure
and data updates, operational readiness, support ownership, adoption evidence,
and feedback into the next release.

## Management information and records

The governed record set should include the approved charter, requirements and
acceptance criteria, option appraisal, estimate basis, funding decision, RAID
and issue logs, dependencies, procurement record, stakeholder and community
engagement, decisions and changes, schedule and status reports, inspection and
GIS lineage, commissioning, training and handover, benefit measures, and
lessons learned.

The live app's downloadable evidence pack contains:

- the filtered asset scope and priority-ordered work programme;
- the multi-year capital screen and explicit assumptions;
- three option postures and five sensitivity cases;
- the indicative funding-source reconciliation;
- the technical/customer service-level catalogue and asset-pressure position;
- four lifecycle strategies for every service in scope;
- one non-approval decision record per capital candidate;
- risk and benefits registers;
- the delivery roadmap;
- a machine-readable scenario manifest; and
- a portable decision brief.

## Conditions before approval

1. Validate current condition, intervention alternatives, treatment scope,
   service consequence, estimate basis, and accountable owner for every
   scheduled asset.
2. Complete safety, accessibility, climate, environmental, equity, statutory,
   and community-impact screens.
3. Confirm whole-life economics, funding eligibility, reserve and debt policy,
   annual cash flow, procurement route, delivery capacity, dependencies,
   permits, and construction windows.
4. Resolve all material source exceptions and preserve the requirement,
   decision, change, approval, GIS, and asset-record trail.
5. Return the preferred option, sensitivities, residual risks, engagement
   evidence, and benefits plan to the accountable approval body.
